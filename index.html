Brief research summary (click to expand)

IoT consists of a distributed network of resource-constrained nodes at the edge. Taken together, these nodes can serve as a computing and storage infrastructure for blockchain mining at the edge. Mining is energy-intensive, especially for low-power IoT boards.

We ran experiments across different block difficulty levels and hash functions, recording the energy and time each IoT board consumed. The result is an empirical relation for blockchain mining on constrained nodes, which helps choose the right board in a resource-limited setting. Using this relation, we proposed a strategy for selecting the nodes that use the least energy and time as mining cores, formulated through Pareto non-dominance.

The work extends to edge/fog computing and blockchain-enabled IoT applications. I aim to publish in reputed IEEE journals and conferences, collaborate on interdisciplinary and externally funded projects, and turn research outcomes into patents and real deployments.
